## Comment 1

[A] There is a lack of civil engineering input in the assessment of the performance of the structures investigated. It would be instructive if information were provided on the design basis of the structures, and their performance relative to engineering expectation.

**Response:**
Thank you for this comment. The revised Methods, Results, Discussion and Table 2 describe the documented design basis and reconstruction scope and relate the observed performance to the intended engineering functions.

The Methods identify Yokoe's original design basis, the cause of the 2016 P3 foundation failure, the targeted reconstruction and the functional objectives of Mashiki widening:

"Kumamoto Prefecture's bridge-restoration report identifies Yokoe as a 200 m long, 8 m wide steel girder-and-truss bridge completed in 1979 under the 1972 Specifications for Highway Bridges. It attributes the 2016 P3 foundation failure and approximately 2.5 m settlement to liquefaction and reduced lateral ground resistance. Reconstruction uses a PC-well foundation at P3 designed for Level 2 ground motion, with repairs to retained superstructure and bearings 33. At Mashiki, the two-to-four-lane widening aims to improve traffic flow, pedestrian safety and disaster-prevention function after collapsed buildings blocked the earlier road 34."
(Page 15, Lines 312–319)

The Results report the damage and repair observations alongside the reopening comparison:

"The official 2026 restoration release documents pier settlement and an abutment joint level difference, with settlement countermeasures and joint repairs in progress 18."
(Page 6, Lines 103–105)

The Discussion relates the operational finding to the specific engineering target:

"The reconstruction targets P3 foundation performance under Level 2 ground motion. Repairs are still necessary in 2026, but traffic returns much sooner; this demonstrates faster operational restoration rather than verified attainment of the P3 design target."
(Page 10, Lines 192–195)

The Discussion also defines the performance evidence assessed for each intervention:

"In this audit, structural reconstruction is evaluated through documented damage, repair work, and matched reopening endpoints, whereas corridor-capacity expansion is evaluated through the expanded route space and observed passage."
(Page 11, Lines 214–217)

Table 2 specifies the P3 reconstruction target and repairs to retained components. The manuscript cites the official reconstruction report, the 2026 restoration release and the governor's briefing on widening objectives. The reconstruction report is included in the References:

"Kumamoto Prefecture. Summary of bridge disaster restoration based on experience from the Kumamoto earthquake (restoration construction works) [in Japanese]. (Kumamoto Prefecture, 2021)."
(Page 32, Lines 118–120)

## Comment 2

[B] The authors note rightly that the principal identification limit is the absence of a credible counterfactual. This paper addresses a single sequence of seismic events. How would the inferences have differed if there were some variation in the seismic sequence?
This raises the question as to what else might have happened? Suppose that the second major earthquake had been larger, e.g. M7.5? To support the robustness of the conclusions, the authors might explore some downward counterfactuals - alternative realizations of the past where things turned for the worse. Under what circumstances might there have been serious damage in the 2026 mainshock?

The usefulness of this paper, and its generalization to other seismic environments, would be enhanced by broadening the context of this study to include such downward counterfactuals.

## Comment 3

- A major concern, from a reviewer working on earthquakes engineering,relates to the comparison of seismic demand between the two earthquake episodes. The authors use Modified Mercalli Intensity (MMI) and peak ground acceleration (PGA) to characterize the shaking experienced by the investigated infrastructure. At Yokoe Bridge, both indicators suggest stronger shaking in 2026 than in 2016, while at Mashiki the two indicators lead to different interpretations. However, PGA and macroseismic intensity are not sufficient by themselves to establish the equivalence or relative severity of seismic demand for specific infrastructure components. Bridge response can depend significantly on spectral acceleration, ground-motion duration, and frequency content. Please advise on these ?

**Response:**
Thank you for highlighting the distinction between local ground motion and component-specific seismic demand. The revised Abstract, Results, Discussion, Methods, Figure 2 and Tables 1–3 distinguish the individual ground-motion metrics, add a three-period spectral comparison, and describe the restoration findings as operational contrasts.

The added analysis compares 5%-damped pseudo-spectral acceleration at 0.3, 1.0 and 3.0 seconds from the archived USGS ShakeMap products at identical within-case sampling coordinates across events. The 2026/2016 ratios are 1.432, 1.838 and 2.909 at Yokoe, and 0.806, 0.743 and 0.695 at Mashiki. Table 2 reports the corresponding event-specific values, and Table 3 aligns the findings and interpretation limits with these comparisons. The Methods describe the sampling support, unit conversion, grid spacing and product versions; the Discussion explains that differences in model generations, prediction equations and resolution prevent interpreting these ratios as a harmonized event-only contrast. Ground-motion duration, complete frequency content and bridge modal properties are not assessed. Figure 2 and its caption retain the restoration-time comparison while removing the unqualified stronger-shaking description.
